## The Crisis

While Case 07 covers the Petrobras dimension of Operation Car Wash, the Latin American regional contagion of Lava Jato is a distinct and arguably more important case study for MINERBA's purposes — because it demonstrates what happens when a single company's corruption becomes a continental governance event affecting every energy and infrastructure market simultaneously.

The vehicle of contagion was **Odebrecht S.A.** — Brazil's largest construction conglomerate, and the dominant contractor for energy infrastructure projects (refineries, hydroelectric dams, pipelines, LNG terminals) across Latin America. Odebrecht had built energy and infrastructure projects in 28 countries. It had won those projects through systematic bribery.

In December 2016, Odebrecht entered a guilty plea with the US Department of Justice, admitting to paying **$788 million in bribes** across 12 countries over approximately 15 years. The plea agreement named officials, political parties, and intermediaries across the hemisphere. It was the largest corporate plea deal in history.

The named countries included: Brazil, Venezuela, Argentina, Colombia, Peru, Mexico, Ecuador, Guatemala, Panama, the Dominican Republic, Angola, and Mozambique.

## Country-by-Country Political Detonations

### Peru
The Odebrecht bribery scandal implicated every living Peruvian president from the previous two decades:
- **Ollanta Humala:** Arrested and briefly imprisoned; wife Nadine Heredia also charged
- **Alejandro Toledo:** Arrested in the US on Peruvian extradition request; had allegedly received $20 million in bribes
- **Pedro Pablo Kuczynski:** Resigned from the presidency in 2018 while facing impeachment; house arrest
- **Alan García:** Died by suicide in April 2019 as police arrived to arrest him

Peru's energy sector — where Odebrecht had built major projects including the Interoceánica highway and Gasoducto Sur Peruano — was thrown into regulatory chaos as contracts were reviewed and cancelled.

### Colombia
Former President Juan Manuel Santos's 2014 election campaign received approximately $1 million in illicit Odebrecht contributions. Multiple senators and regional governors were implicated. The country's infrastructure and energy programme — built substantially around Odebrecht contracts — faced years of project delays and renegotiations.

### Ecuador
Odebrecht paid approximately $33.5 million in bribes to Ecuadorian officials. Vice President Jorge Glas was convicted and imprisoned. Multiple energy and infrastructure contracts were suspended for review. The controversy added to Ecuador's already troubled energy sector (see also Cases L1 and L3).

### Venezuela
While the full scope of Odebrecht's activities in Venezuela remained partially opaque due to the Maduro government's opacity, the company had extensive PDVSA-connected contracts. The Lava Jato investigators' access to Venezuela was severely limited by the political environment.

### Argentina
Odebrecht's Argentine operations implicated officials connected to infrastructure projects, contributing to the broader political volatility of the Macri-era governance debates.

## The Communications Disaster: No Coordinated Regional Response

The defining PR failure of the Lava Jato regional contagion was the complete absence of any coordinated regional communications response. This was a structural impossibility: multiple companies (Odebrecht, Petrobras, Braskem, and dozens of local contractors) were implicated simultaneously across a dozen countries, each with different legal systems, different political dynamics, different media environments, and different phases of the investigation.

What actually happened:
- **Each firm issued separate, contradictory statements** calibrated to its own legal exposure in each jurisdiction
- **Each country's politicians responded based on their domestic political interests** — some embraced investigation, others obstructed it
- **Odebrecht's plea deal named names globally before local investigations were complete** — meaning that in multiple countries, the first public statement from a named company or official was a denial of something Odebrecht had already admitted to in US court documents
- **The plea deal's 'cooperation' provisions** — which required Odebrecht to provide further evidence on additional corruption in exchange for sentence reduction — created a continuing drip of new revelations over multiple years, making it impossible for any company in the ecosystem to declare "this is behind us"

---

## The Odebrecht Plea Deal as Communications Event

Odebrecht's guilty plea was itself a masterclass in adversarial communications management by the US DOJ. The plea agreement was structured to:
1. Require maximum cooperation — Odebrecht's executives had to testify against counterparties
2. Name specific countries, officials, and amounts — creating instantaneous news stories in every jurisdiction simultaneously
3. Be filed in a US court — giving it global legal and journalistic credibility regardless of what local governments said

For any company or government official named, the immediate communications problem was existential: a US federal court document, publicly filed, stated that they had taken bribes. Any denial had to compete with that specific, documented, judicially filed claim.

## Outcome & Legacy

- At least **10 heads of state or former presidents** across Latin America were indicted, convicted, imprisoned, or forced to resign
- The energy and infrastructure pipeline across the region was disrupted for years as contracts were reviewed and cancelled
- New anti-corruption legislation was enacted in Brazil, Peru, Colombia, and Ecuador
- International energy companies faced dramatically heightened due diligence requirements when entering LatAm markets
- The regional contagion demonstrated that supply chain corruption — through shared contractors — can create simultaneous crisis exposure across an entire region

---

## Key PR Lessons

1. **In Latin America, energy and infrastructure corruption crises rapidly become continental political events.** They cannot be managed at the corporate level alone.
2. **A co-conspirator's plea deal eliminates narrative control across all named parties simultaneously.** There is no preparation adequate for this scenario except not being in the scheme.
3. **Shared contractors are a shared reputational risk.** Energy companies that use the same major contractors as state energy firms inherit a fraction of those contractors' reputational exposure.
4. **Regional communications infrastructure must exist before regional crises.** No company in the Lava Jato ecosystem had coordinated regional PR capacity; they improvised independently and incoherently.
5. **Investigation timelines are measured in years, not months.** The Lava Jato regional impact sustained intense media pressure from 2014 to 2019 and beyond — no company can wait out a five-year news cycle.
6. **Political cycle awareness is essential.** In multiple countries, the scandal's impact was amplified by proximity to election cycles; in others, elections brought governments that stalled investigations.

## Latin America Application (Structural Risks for New Market Entrants)

| Risk Category | Specific Risk | MINERBA Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Contractor due diligence | Energy companies share contractors with state firms; a contractor's corruption implicates all clients | Conduct enhanced anti-corruption due diligence on all contractors; include anti-corruption warranties in contracts with audit rights |
| State partner exposure | Partnering with state energy firms means sharing their corruption risk profile | Assess state partner's current and historical corruption exposure before JV; include contractual exit provisions |
| Political cycle mapping | Scandal impact varies dramatically by political phase | Map electoral calendars across operating countries; assess which governments are investigating vs. obstructing and why |
| Multi-country crisis capacity | No single PR firm has adequate simultaneous coverage across 12 LatAm countries | Pre-build a network of in-country crisis communications partners before operations begin; test coordination annually |
| DOJ/FCPA exposure | US jurisdiction applies to any company with US dollar transactions or US-listed securities | Treat FCPA compliance as a communications asset, not just a legal obligation; publish compliance programme details |
